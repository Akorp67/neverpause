The Hot Sleepers
Guide

What to check on a label before you buy another "cooling" anything. Plain language, no hype.

Why so much sleepwear lets you down

When you heat up at night, your body is letting heat and moisture out through your skin. That's your body doing its job.

The problem is what's sitting on top of it. A lot of sleepwear sold as "cooling" is synthetic material. It can move liquid sweat around, but it holds heat and moisture in, and once it's damp it sticks. So your pajamas end up working against you.

"...and still have to change pajamas twice a night. It's maddening."A woman on r/Menopause
Be careful with
✕
Polyester "cooling" fabricIt takes in very little moisture itself. Feels fine at bedtime, can feel clammy by 3am.
✕
The word "bamboo" on its ownUsually means bamboo viscose, which is a rayon. The label is selling the plant, not how it performs. Check the weight and the cut instead.
✕
Thick, heavy fabricWeight matters as much as fibre. If it feels like a sweatshirt in your hand, it'll sleep like one.
✕
Tight, clingy cutsFabric pressed against damp skin leaves no room for air.
Look for
✓
LyocellA fibre derived from wood pulp. It absorbs and releases moisture and feels cool to the touch.
✓
A light weightLook for a low GSM (grams per square meter). It's how fabric weight is measured. The lower the number, the lighter the fabric, and lighter fabric lets heat move.
✓
A loose, drapey cutFabric that hangs off the body instead of being clingy or tight fitting.
✓
Real openingsSide slits and a racerback aren't decoration. They let air in at the hips and off the back.
